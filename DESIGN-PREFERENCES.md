# Matthew's Design Taste — LLM Reference & Pre-Flight Check

**Purpose:** Run through this *before* showing Matthew a design, layout, interface, product concept, or piece of copy and asking "is this good?" Most of his likely objections are already written down here. Catch them yourself first.

**Source:** Derived from 619 conversations (10,075 messages, Dec 2024 – Aug 2026). Every quoted line was programmatically verified to appear in one of Matthew's own 5,048 messages — anything the assistant said back to him was discarded, so nothing here is Claude's opinion reflected at him. Where he never stated a preference, this document says so rather than guessing.

**Who he is (context that explains the taste):** A designer who codes. Freelance brand/print work (political campaigns, nonprofits, event posters), native iOS apps (Happy Eat), and physical hardware (Talkbox — an ambient ESP32 voicebox; Clicky). Bauhaus and deconstructivist leanings. Reads Bret Victor and Engelbart. Wants to run his own research lab, not a startup.

**Weighting:** Taste evolved. 2026 material (Talkbox, campaign branding, Clicky, brutalist web) reflects current taste most accurately. Early-2025 SwiftUI material is still valid but older. When two entries conflict, prefer the more recent.

## 0. The one-line version

> Design is a way of *thinking* that solves real human problems — and it should still be beautiful, bold, and desirable. Windows, not mirrors. Presence, not performance. Concrete, not vague. Never glaze him.

## 2. Design philosophy

**Design is problem-solving, not decoration.** Stated more forcefully and more often than anything else in the corpus:

> *"design IS the way we innovate and THAT IS THE EXACT SKILL I WANT TO HONE — not better coding, not better entrepreneurship — BETTER DESIGN TO SOLVE SOCIETY'S WICKED PROBLEMS"*
> *"His entire approach to design is NOT about form or art at all, it's about DESIGN AS A WAY OF THINKING"*
> *"My creative process wasn't about making things look good – it was about solving problems."*

**But he genuinely loves beauty — do not over-correct into ugly utilitarianism:**

> *"design IS MY GUILTY PLEASURE — i WANT to make something desirable (but i also want to change the world) — and design is both the means of getting there and, honestly, the ends i love"*

**How he reconciles the two:**

> *"Her work is beautiful—but not because every pixel is perfect... it's beautiful because it touches real problems so tastefully."*

**Corollaries:**
- Judge **systems**, not artifacts. *"which system is better (first, same) or second and third and fourth together"*; *"i don't wanna just extend a half assed brand"*
- Logos need versatility. *"there is literally no versatility or scalable stuff with her current logo"*
- Process is part of the work. He admires *"an image of all her iterations for a Puma design project."*
- Break rules with intent. *"the solutions to the world's biggest problems won't come from an engineer unwilling to break the rules"*; *"I have always liked making ugly art... I loved how it didn't make sense."*
- Interdisciplinary or nothing. *"i dread a one-dimensional education"*; rejects both soulless CS and *"too artsy"* design programs.

## 3. Visual & aesthetic preferences

### Strip it down
- *"STRIP THIS DIAGRAM DOWN TO THE BARE BONES... COMPLETELY MINIMALIST BLACK AND WHITE USE THIS VERY CLEAN STYLE AS REFERENCE"*
- *"bro this is so ugly please use that generic latex scientific look it is supposed to look SCIENTIFIC - BASIC LIKE THIS BRO MAKE THE LAYOUT CONDENSED AND CLEAN DRASTICALY SIMPLIFY"*
- *"ok you're trying to cram in too much - cut unecessary detail"*
- Condense for the real output medium: *"condense the layout so it isn't as wide and fits onto a paper better"*

### But bold, never timid
- Type must carry equal weight to the imagery: *"it doesn't match the boldness of the cat. like the middle option in my previous draft was bold. this is just it says steve raspe."*
- *"BE DRAMATIC - LIKE I SWEAR TOP SIDE YELLOW WHITE BOTTOM SIDE RICH SKY BLUE - I JUST WANT TO SEE A CONTRAST"*
- He backs the bold option even against client pressure: *"i adore the middle. if kurt or steve don't im crashing out"*
- He notices density creeping in: *"it gets a bit busy"*
- Obvious ideas bore him: *"i mean cat is easy bait though… if anyone was creative they would put a cat on it. can't just be me"*

### Named styles he has actually asked for
- **Brutalist + Frutiger Aero** (Aug 2026, most recent explicit spec): *"raw brutalist web design — monospace type, stark black-on-white, thick 2–3px solid borders, zero border-radius, exposed grid lines, brutal high contrast, utilitarian and unpolished-on-purpose. Blend in accents of Frutiger Aero: glossy glass buttons with reflective highlights, aqua-blue gradients"*
- **Bauhaus** — self-described *"Bauhaus afficionado"*
- **Deconstructivist** — *"Half of my insta feed is deconstructivist graphic design"*

### Form
- Rounded over angular, in diagrams: *"turn hexagons into rounded rectangles"*
- Soft, tactile, warm in objects: *"a small glowing ornament with soft corners and impish orange accents you could hold in your palm"*
- Architectural, not gadget-like: *"The casing feels architectural, like a window, something that feels like an addendum to your space"*
- Small and endearing beats grand: *"a tiny record player feels cute enough"*
- Three-dimensional over flat: *"The flower felt too flat, so I layered a physical doily on top"*

### Color
- Bold and deliberate — *"a colorful little voicebox"*
- Reject genre clichés: *"i was stuck in 'californian' or 'politically good' colors but now im here idk if its practical but oh well i like it"*
- Ambient light should read as material, not illumination: *"i want it to look good in AMBIENT settings - not like light but like just material color"*
- Let users own it: *"color can be chosen by people or time themselves for customization since people like that"*

### Imperfection is a feature
- *"intentionally allowing wrinkles because flattening them felt too perfect"*
- *"using physical blending that rewards careful looking rather than digital effects"*
- Prefers ambiguous symbols to explicit labels: *"ambiguous symbols represent our identities more naturally than labels or words"*

### Motion
- Default register: *"Motion: subtle & tasteful."*
- Organic, never mechanical: *"ripples in beautifully that's lively like liquid glass"*; *"gentle morphing sunset color pattern that feels like a window"*
- Life beats technical correctness: *"the magic of light is in the BLOOM so give life to THAT"*
- He *will* choreograph precisely when he cares: *"after 2 seconds fade the great job TO THE LEFT and the 8 days completed IN FROM THE RIGHT, so it looks linear"*, *"increment the days completed number with a nice number increment up animation... shoot confetti!"*
- Anchor stable elements while content moves: *"z stack it and leave it in the body? but make the text change with a fade animation as everything else changes"*
- Complexity for its own sake gets cut: *"screw all the super complicated blooming stuff"*

## 4. Interaction & UX

- **Physical affordances that signal intent.** *"like the mechanism of those old cassette players or cd players that has that click to be like okay im not touch phone i just talk if i want"*
- **Binary physical controls over menus.** *"it can be simpler even just a knob that's talk or be and it both mutes and deafens"*
- **Ambient and continuous, not sporadic and impulsive.** *"requires a new form to hold and look at and a different mode of interaction (continued, ambient, physical vs sporadic, impulsive, and digital)"*
- **Always-on presence, not summoned alerts.** *"It gently pulses when someone far away talks to you. It sits on your desk, permanently."*
- **Remove ritual friction.** *"No ringing. No scheduling"*; scheduled interaction *"felt too deliberate, too clinical."*
- **Full-duplex and gestural, not turn-based text.** *"not just a what do u think but a conversational full duplex and not just in text but something that can point and circle etc"*
- **Give agency with legible feedback.** *"brightness depending on how willing u are + pulse depending on how willing someone ELSE is... if you REALLY need to focus you can pretty much just turn it off completely"*
- **Hide complexity behind one clean surface.** *"i only ever have to call to volume basically it handles out on its own"*
- **Make participation feel like a quest.** *"if i just got an email saying 'welcome! here's 3 events idk sign up if you want' then thats lame... but if its like a quest... ooooh"*
- **Physical artifacts must feel good in the hand.** *"no i want a real passport thing something that feels GOOD"*
- **Don't ship the broken part.** *"our server is NOT fun lol so... might hurt more than help to get them in there"*
- **Dark mode is checked.** *"dark mode is so ugly help"*
- Concrete UI details he has specified: selected state as blue fill + white text; palette-matched controls over system materials (*"can you make the picker less ugly lol its system materials on yellow background — better would be using lightYellow and whiteYellow"*).

## 5. Copy & voice

**Order of operations — he stated it explicitly:**
> *"clarity, prompt alignment, and readability first! then power"*

- **Concrete beats vague.** *"closer still doesn't explain that its like talking to a friend"*
- **Punchy and short.** *"make these two paragraphs punchier - clear - just 50 words each"*; *"four punchy sentences"*; *"keep it dead easy - like three sentences and pretty simple too - but power"*
- **Understated.** *"oh too much lol keep it understated"*
- **No purple prose, no pretension.** *"better word than gloaming idk to less 'purple prose'"*; *"idk ache line feels a bit prtneious lol"*; *"it feels too pretentious and abstract"*
- **No marketing glaze.** *"monolith feels like glazing"*
- **No padding or false politeness.** *"no need to say thanks or pad ending idk"*
- **Don't explain the feeling.** *"dont be on the nose make the feeling speak for itself"*
- **Leave it open.** *"leave some room for me to really digest jayden in a way that isnt neat"*
- **Vary the pacing.** *"can you revise the pacing of the syntax / add imagery where possible to make the story beat properly? like everything's kind of the same length right now"*
- **Imagery must earn its place.** *"the imagery should be LEGITIMATELY GOOD. - not hollow pretty"*
- **Narrative, not lists.** *"stylistically id much rather prefer a coherent thought narrative"*; *"i feel like 'meh' projects just ruin the intensity or power"*
- **Ground it in real memory.** *"add a nostalgic memory of me remember those talk tubes in playgrounds + how i wanted to bring that back"* — and he will call out invented detail: *"unfortunately no good imagery - i was just on my computer at 1am watching stanford lectures on youtube lol"*
- **Be honest about stage.** *"can i be honest about not having tested current proto yet"*
- **Preserve his voice.** *"POWER + CLARITY FIRST - DON'T OVEREDIT PRESERVE TONE"*; *"maintain my voice... be down to earth."*

## 6. How to work with him

**Never glaze.** The single most repeated process complaint in the corpus:
> *"WHY ARE YOU GLAZING ME GIVE ME SOMETHING CONSTRUCTIVE"*
> *"BRUH STOP GLAZIN ME"*
> *"Be brutally honest! If something feels off, confusing, or unnecessary—tell me."*
> *"you don't have to sugar coat"*

**Other working rules:**
- **Give numbered options**, don't prescribe one answer. *"give me some options"* — he picks fast and decisively (*"HOLY I LOVE 3"*).
- **Ship the whole file.** *"Give me the complete file in one go so I can preview it immediately."*
- **Change only what he asked.** *"please dont put it in a scroll view — JUST MAKE IT SMALLER IF ITS ON AN SE AND LEAVE EVERYTHING ELSE ALONE PLEASE"*
- **Skip the lecture when he wants the fix.** *"ok bro highk just give me the fix no pedagogy for now"*
- **Clarity over cleverness in code.** *"I'm learning, so favor clarity over cleverness."*
- **Keep code short.** *"MAKE THE CODE AS SHORT AS POSSIBLE"*
- **Scaffold ugly on purpose when he'll style it himself.** *"SUPER SIMPLE BAREBONES NATIVE UGLY — leave the ui prettification to me - your job is to write this as concisely and neat as possible"*
- **He wants to see it run.** *"thERE WE GO - I SEE IT!!!"*
- **Talk to the human before designing.** *"let me CALL HIM FIRST SO I CAN TALK ABOUT IT I EOULD RATHER SOEND MORE TIME AND CALL THEN WORK OFF LF THIS"*
- **Agree the goals up front, then judge against them — not against vibes.** This is how he thinks design should be evaluated, and it's the single most useful thing to do before asking him "is this good?": *"the point of a cap isn't to get 'feedback' it's to establish common ground for a design so [the client] doesn't judge the final logo based on aesthetics but on did we accomplish the goals i agreed on with the designer"*
- **Attach the rationale to the work.** *"if i could send it to steve with design rationale that would also be great so he has better context to react to than pure aesthetics"*
- **Caps lock = intensity, not anger.** Heavy caps means he cares. Match the energy; don't get delicate.

## 7. Auto-reject list

If the design does any of these, fix it before showing him:

1. **Mirror, not window** — makes the user look at themselves, perform, or optimize a self-image.
2. **Attention-extractive** — streaks, algorithmic feeds, engagement loops, notification bait.
3. **A chatbot wrapper** — *"ok these are lame its all ai apps."*
4. **Pretty but purposeless** — visual polish with no real problem underneath.
5. **Timid** — safe palette, meek type, no contrast, no personality.
6. **Overstuffed** — *"you're trying to cram in too much."*
7. **Genre-cliché color** — the "obviously political blue," the expected wellness green.
8. **Type that undersells the imagery** — bold graphic, limp wordmark.
9. **Vague copy** — abstract benefit language, "monolith"-style glaze, hedges, padded closings.
10. **A resume/feature list** where a narrative belongs.
11. **Neat bow endings** that resolve everything.
12. **Un-versatile logo** that breaks at other sizes or formats.
13. **A half-fixed system** — patching a weak brand instead of repairing it.
14. **Scheduled/ceremonial interaction** where ambient would do.
15. **Unchecked dark mode.**
16. **Over-engineered scope** — extra abstraction, scroll views, and features he didn't ask for.

## 8. Known tensions — resolve them the way he does

These look contradictory. They aren't. Getting them backwards is the most likely way to misread him.

| Apparent conflict | Resolution |
|---|---|
| "Minimalist black and white" vs. "colorful, bold, dramatic contrast" | **Minimal structure, bold accent.** Strip the system to bare bones, then let one or two elements be loud. Never busy *and* loud. |
| "Design isn't about making things look good" vs. "design is my guilty pleasure" | Beauty is never the *justification*, but it's always the *standard*. Lead with the problem; deliver it beautifully. |
| "SUPER SIMPLE BAREBONES NATIVE UGLY" vs. detailed animation specs | Depends on ownership. If **he** will style it, give plain scaffolding. If **you** are delivering the finished thing, make it beautiful. Ask which mode you're in. |
| "Brutalist, zero border-radius" vs. "turn hexagons into rounded rectangles" | Medium-specific. Brutalism for web/marketing surfaces; soft rounded forms for diagrams, physical objects, and app UI. |
| "Subtle & tasteful motion" vs. "shoot confetti!" | Ambient states stay quiet. **Earned moments** — completion, milestone, first success — get expressive payoff. |
| "Imperfection is beautiful" vs. "make it clean" | Imperfection is *chosen* (a wrinkle, a hand-made mark). Sloppiness is not. Clean structure, human texture. |

## 9. Pre-flight checklist

Run this before asking "is this good?" Anything unchecked is something to fix or to flag explicitly.

**Purpose**
- [ ] Names a real problem a real person described — not an imagined pain point.
- [ ] Directs attention outward (window), not inward (mirror).
- [ ] Doesn't extract attention, isolate, or gamify for its own sake.
- [ ] Offers an alternative rather than merely blocking or critiquing.
- [ ] The medium (app / web / print / hardware) is justified by the problem.

**Form**
- [ ] Stripped to bare bones; nothing crammed in.
- [ ] Bold somewhere — real contrast, real personality, not timid.
- [ ] Typography matches the weight of the imagery.
- [ ] Palette isn't the genre default.
- [ ] Structure minimal, accents loud — not both busy.
- [ ] Fits its actual output medium (paper size, screen, physical scale).
- [ ] Dark mode checked, if applicable.

**Interaction**
- [ ] Ambient and continuous where possible, not scheduled or ceremonial.
- [ ] Physical/tactile affordance if it's hardware; a click that means something.
- [ ] Complexity hidden behind one clean surface.
- [ ] User has agency and legible feedback.
- [ ] Motion subtle by default; expressive only at earned moments.

**Copy**
- [ ] Clear first, powerful second.
- [ ] Concrete, not abstract.
- [ ] Punchy and short — cut it again.
- [ ] No glaze, no hedging, no padded closing.
- [ ] Shows the feeling instead of naming it.
- [ ] Narrative, not a feature list.

**Delivery**
- [ ] Complete file, previewable immediately.
- [ ] Only what he asked for changed.
- [ ] Options offered where there's a real choice — numbered.
- [ ] **Goals stated up front, and the work argued against those goals** — not offered up for a raw aesthetic verdict.
- [ ] Design rationale attached, so he reacts to reasoning rather than vibes.
- [ ] Your honest critique included, including what you think is weak.
- [ ] No glazing. Say what's wrong with it.
